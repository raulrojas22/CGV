Who it is for

Biologists, bioinformaticians, educators and students who want a guided visual workflow without losing useful detail.

Designed for

  • Comparative gene, exon, CDS, UTR, and transcript structure analysis
  • Aligned exon-level relationships across transcripts or organisms
  • Interactive metrics, sequence composition, genomic context, and annotations
  • Preloaded references plus custom genome and annotation uploads
  • Publication figures, tables, sequences, and reproducible session files

Not designed for

  • Differential expression, isoform switching, or RNA-seq quantification
  • Variant calling, SNP / INDEL analysis, or de novo assembly
  • Whole-genome browsing or large-region navigation
  • Phylogenetic inference or protein 3D structure prediction
  • High-throughput, genome-wide batch screening

Frequently asked

What is CGeV best used for?

Gene-centered structural exploration: exon and intron organization, CDS and UTR structure, transcript variants, sequence metrics, genomic context, and cross-species conservation in one guided workspace.

Multi-Gene or Cross-Species?

Choose Multi-Gene to inspect several genes within one organism, compare paralogs, or align isoforms from one loaded gene. Choose Cross-Species to compare one gene across organisms and evaluate structural conservation.

Which synteny sequence type should I use?

Use Translated CDS for more divergent comparisons, CDS nucleotide for closely related coding sequences, and Exon for full exon-level comparison including UTR segments when available.

What can I access from a gene card?

Structural metrics, sequence composition, transcript information, genomic context, GO annotations, recent literature, promoter extraction, sequence actions, and export options.

How do I export or save my work?

Export individual or composed figures, ZIP batches, CSV tables, FASTA sequences, or a complete .rds session. On Web, Share creates an expiring secret read-only report URL; on Desktop it saves the same interactive report as a local self-contained HTML file with its reproducibility ZIP.

How is CGeV different from a genome browser?

Genome browsers prioritize locus navigation. CGeV is narrower and more guided: side-by-side gene structure comparison, transcript and species alignment, analytics, figure composition, and reusable exports.
