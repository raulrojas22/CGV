SECCIÓN 2: Search Modes
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Título: 🔀 Search Modes
Subtítulo: Two complementary workflows with live gene suggestions, alias-aware recovery, and ranked resolution when the same name points to multiple loci.

Search intelligence shared by both workflows:
  • Live gene suggestions built from selected annotations.
  • Ranked ambiguity resolution when one name/alias points to several loci, including stable IDs, coordinates, match roles, and descriptions.
  • Similar local names first, with optional MyGene, NCBI, UniProt, and Ensembl alias recovery.

→ Multi-Gene Search
  Inspect and compare multiple annotated genes within one organism side by side. Ideal for structural review across isoforms and paralogs.
  • Visualize: Compact and Detailed views for all genes.
  • Align transcripts: when a loaded gene has at least two isoforms, select that gene and compare all its transcripts using translated CDS, CDS nucleotide, or exon sequence, transcript ordering, and an identity threshold.
  • Access all 10 analytics chart types, plus batch SVG export.

→ Cross-Species Gene Search
  Compare one gene across multiple organisms with representative models aligned side by side. Designed for cross-species structural analysis.
  • Visualize: Compact and Detailed views for all organisms.
  • Align across species: synteny, LASTZ Blocks, and Multi-PIP to compare exon structure and genomic conservation.
  • Exon-level event labeling: conservation, duplication (1:n, n:1), loss, and partial matches.

SECCIÓN 3: Visualization & Alignment
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Título: 👁 Visualization & Alignment
Subtítulo: Each search mode has two top-level modes: Visualize (explore gene models) and Alignment (compare structures). Within Alignment, choose a strategy and a sequence type.

👁 Visualize mode
Gene structure inspection — available in both search modes.

  Compact
  Minimalist gene structure panel. Fits many genes on screen. Ideal for first-pass inspection.
  Badge: Both modes

  Detailed
  Full annotation with exons, CDS, UTR, strand, coordinates and zoom. Best for thorough single-gene review.
  Badge: Both modes

🔗 Alignment mode
Compare structures across transcripts (Multi-Gene) or across species (Cross-Species). Alignment mode becomes available once the required data is loaded.

  Multi-Gene: Align Transcripts
  When a gene has two or more transcript isoforms, align them with comparative synteny to identify which exon corresponds to which.
  • Synteny — pairwise representative alignment with exon correspondence ribbons and event labels (conserved, split, fusion, loss, partial).
  ℹ Available when at least one loaded gene has multiple transcripts.

  Cross-Species: Align Across Organisms
  Compare one gene's representative transcript across organisms with three alignment strategies:
  • Synteny — representative transcript alignment with annotated exon correspondence ribbons and event-type labels.
  • LASTZ Blocks — local pairwise alignment blocks showing conserved regions between the query and each reference.
  • Multi-PIP — Percent Identity Plot across the genomic window for conservation patterns.
  ℹ Available after loading at least two organism tracks.

🔀 Sequence types (Synteny alignment)
All Synteny alignments use Needleman-Wunsch global alignment. Choose the sequence representation that best fits your evolutionary distance:

  Translated CDS — Aligns protein translations. Best for detecting conserved coding regions across divergent genomes. [BLOSUM62]
  CDS Nucleotide — Aligns nucleotide CDS directly. Useful for closely related species where nucleotide identity is high. [EDNAFULL]
  Exon — Aligns full exon sequences including UTRs. Captures structural variation beyond CDS, suitable for lncRNA comparisons. [EDNAFULL]

  ℹ Configurable window sizes for LASTZ Blocks and Multi-PIP: gene body, ±5 kb, ±10 kb, ±25 kb, ±50 kb.

SECCIÓN 4: Understanding Results
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Título: 📊 Understanding Results
Subtítulo: CGV returns multiple output layers. Each adds a different level of depth to your analysis.

  Gene cards
  • One card per gene with structure plot (exons, CDS, UTR, strand, zoom, hoverable coordinates).
  • Metrics: transcript length, CDS length, protein length (aa), GC%, exon/intron counts, biotype.
  • Quick-action links for alias resolution and direct sequence actions.

  Gene card popups
  • Metrics popup — nucleotide composition donut, isoform comparison chart, genomic context scatter.
  • GO Annotations — Gene Ontology terms from local files or live Ensembl lookup.
  • Papers — recent publications via Europe PMC.
  • Promoter — upstream region FASTA (100–5,000 bp) with coordinate export.

  Summary Table
  • Sortable and filterable. Columns: gene, transcript, chromosome, strand, exon count, lengths, organism.
  • Filter or rank results quickly before opening chart panels.
  • Export as CSV for downstream analysis.

10 comparative analytics chart types:
  Architecture | Exons & Introns | Sequence | Genomic Context | Exon Lengths
  Intron Lengths | Scatter | Heatmap | Radar | Correlations

Up to 11 focused gene/isoform charts:
  Gene/Transcript Structure | Exon Map | Intron Map | Sequence Composition
  Isoform Lengths | Isoform Architecture | Exonic vs Intronic bp | Isoform Sequence

SECCIÓN 8: Why CGV was built
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Título: 💡 Why CGV was built
Subtítulo: Comparative gene structure analysis made easier to inspect, explain, and share — without jumping between browsers, scripts, and static plots.

  The problem
  Genome browsers are great for locus inspection, but not for clear side-by-side gene structure comparison across species.

  The workflow gap
  Search, alignment, inspection, and export are usually spread across several tools. CGV unifies them.

  Who CGV is for
  Biologists, bioinformaticians, and students who want a guided visual workflow instead of code-heavy pipelines.

Título: ℹ Scope
Subtítulo: CGV is focused on gene-centered structure interpretation. Clear scope helps pair CGV with the right upstream or downstream tools.

  ✓ Designed for

  • Preloaded references with custom genome and annotation uploads

  ✗ Not designed for
  • Differential expression, isoform switching, or abundance testing
  • Variant calling, SNP/INDEL analysis, RNA-seq quantification
  • Whole-genome browsing or large region navigation
  • De novo genome or transcriptome assembly
  • Phylogenetic inference or protein 3D structure prediction
  • High-throughput genome-wide batch analysis

SECCIÓN 11: Troubleshooting & Tips
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Título: 🔧 Troubleshooting & Tips
Subtítulo: Most issues come from query spelling, organism selection, or annotation structure. This section covers the most common checks.

  Data inputs
  • Uploads require a genome file in .fa, .fa.gz, or .2bit plus a matching annotation in .gff3 or .gtf.
  • You can mix available references with uploaded organisms in the same analysis.
  • CGV currently ships with more than two dozen preloaded organism references.

  If a gene is missing
  • Check gene symbol spelling and letter case.
  • Confirm that at least one organism is selected before running the search.
  • For uploaded data, verify that the annotation contains the expected gene identifiers and associated transcript features.
  [Logos: MyGene | NCBI Gene | UniProt | Ensembl]

  Performance tips
  • Start with fewer organisms or genes, then scale up once the first view looks correct.
  • Use the summary table to narrow what you inspect before opening all analytics panels.
  • Reorder charts when the plot count grows so comparisons stay readable.

  Sessions and display settings
  • Export your current work session as .rds when you want to resume later.
  • Restore a saved session from Settings to recover plots and configuration.
  • Theme, dark mode, and colorblind options can help readability for dense figure review.

SECCIÓN 12: Frequently Asked Questions
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Título: ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is CGV best used for?
A: CGV is designed for gene-centered structural comparison. It excels at examining exon/intron architecture, comparing representative gene models across species, exploring transcript isoforms, and producing publication-ready figures — all within a single guided interface, without requiring any scripting.

Q: Do I need to install anything before using CGV?
A: The hosted CGV app runs entirely in the browser. CGV Desktop is optional for users who want the complete workspace and reference catalog on their own macOS or Linux computer.

Q: What is CGV Desktop and which systems does it support?
A: CGV Desktop packages the full application and analysis runtime for macOS and Linux. It runs through a private localhost session and stores installed organisms, caches, and workspaces in the user's profile.

Q: When should I choose Multi-Gene Search versus Cross-Species Gene Search?
A: Choose Multi-Gene Search to inspect several genes in one organism, compare paralogs, or align the transcripts of a loaded gene with two or more isoforms. Choose Cross-Species Gene Search to compare one gene across organisms and evaluate structural conservation or divergence.

Q: What does the transcript-level comparative alignment show?
A: In Multi-Gene Search, select a loaded gene with at least two transcripts and compare those isoforms using translated CDS, CDS nucleotide, or full exon sequence. The view reveals shared blocks, transcript-specific regions, alternative exon usage, and coding-structure differences. Transcript order and identity threshold can be adjusted before alignment.

Q: What does the Cross-Species Synteny view show?
A: It selects one representative transcript per organism and aligns them globally using Needleman-Wunsch. The result is displayed as aligned exon blocks connected by ribbons, with each exon-pair labeled by event type:
  • 1:1 — single conserved exon correspondence.
  • 1:n / n:1 — exon split or fusion events.
  • Partial — partial overlap between exons.
  • Lost — exon present in one organism, absent in the other.

Q: What sequence type should I choose for synteny alignment?
A:
  • Translated CDS (BLOSUM62) — recommended for distantly related species where nucleotide identity is low but protein function is conserved.
  • CDS Nucleotide (EDNAFULL) — best for closely related species where the coding sequence at nucleotide level is still similar.
  • Exon (EDNAFULL) — aligns the full exon including UTR regions. Useful for lncRNAs or when UTR conservation is of interest.

Q: What information can I access by clicking on a gene card?
A: Clicking a gene opens a set of popup panels:
  • Metrics — nucleotide composition donut, isoform length comparison, and genomic context scatter.
  • GO Annotations — Gene Ontology terms sourced from local annotation files, with live fallback to Ensembl.
  • Papers — recent publications retrieved from Europe PMC for that gene and organism.
  • Promoter — configurable upstream region (100–5,000 bp) shown as FASTA sequence with coordinate export.

Q: What files can I upload?
A: Genome formats: .fa, .fasta, .fna (plain or gzip-compressed), or .2bit.
  Annotation formats: .gff3 or .gtf.
  The genome and annotation files must correspond to the same organism. You can mix uploaded organisms with references available in the current CGV session.

Q: How do I export or save my work?
A:
  • SVG — individual chart or gene structure plot export.
  • ZIP — batch export of analytics chart SVGs or gene structure plot SVGs.
  • CSV — summary table with lengths, exon/intron counts, GC%, and coordinates.
  • FASTA — gene spans, spliced transcripts, CDS, inferred introns, or configurable promoter regions.
  • Session (.rds) — saves the full workspace. Restore it any time from Settings → Session Management.

Q: Why might a gene not appear in the results?
A:
  • The gene symbol may be misspelled or use an alternative alias not present in the selected annotation.
  • No organism is selected for the current search — at least one must be active.
  • For uploaded data, the annotation file may not contain the expected gene identifier or a matching transcript feature.
  Tip: enable external alias lookup (MyGene, NCBI Gene, UniProt, or Ensembl) in Settings to automatically resolve alternative names and IDs.

Q: How is CGV different from genome browsers or plotting packages?
A: Genome browsers are built for locus navigation and broad genomic context. Plotting packages require scripted figure generation. CGV occupies a different space: guided, gene-first comparison with cross-species alignment, 10 comparative analytics plus up to 11 focused gene/isoform charts, popup information layers, and multiple export formats — all without writing code.

Q: What does CGV not do?
A: CGV is focused on gene structure visualization and comparative analysis. It does not perform: RNA-seq quantification, differential expression testing, variant calling, genome assembly, phylogenetic tree construction, or protein structure prediction. For those workflows, use dedicated tools and bring the results back to CGV for structural context.
